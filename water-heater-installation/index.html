Water heater installation covers more ground than a simple swap. Plenty of Royal Oak, MI projects start with a finished basement or a new addition that needs its own heater. Others are moving a unit out of a cramped closet and into the garage, upgrading to a larger tank for a growing household, or converting from one fuel to another. All of them mean new gas piping, vent routes, circuits, or water lines, planned out before anyone cuts a pipe. Royal Oak Water Heater handles installations of every size, from a single point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Sizing and placement shape most of the outcome. For a tank, we compare first-hour ratings against your household's busiest hour; for a tankless unit, we size by flow rate and winter temperature rise. Location comes next. Gas heaters need a proper vent route and combustion air, heat pump heaters need open space and a condensate drain, and whole-house electric tankless units often need a large dedicated circuit and the panel capacity to match. Put a heater above a finished ceiling and it needs a drain pan; put a gas unit in a garage and code may require a raised platform. You get the trade-offs in plain terms, with no pressure.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a fast one part ways. Our technicians leak-test every gas joint, set vent pitch and clearances to the manufacturer's spec, match the circuit and breaker to the heater, and use dielectric unions or fittings where copper meets steel. The relief valve discharge line runs full size to where it belongs, and expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are installed when code requires them, with the permit handled when needed. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered
  • A remodel that moves the utility space to a new spot
  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water
  • Venting, relief valve piping, or gas connections that do not look right
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Installation?

A new water heater installation is really several trades in one job. The gas piping must be sized for the heater plus every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested until it is leak-free. The vent must meet the manufacturer's rules on diameter, length, pitch, and clearances, or carbon monoxide can find its way indoors. Electric and heat pump units need a circuit, breaker, and wire gauge matched to their load, and whole-house electric tankless units can draw more current than many panels have to spare. On the water side, there is more: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that vary by location.

A professional installation covers the whole job, not just setting the heater. We size by first-hour rating or flow rate, run and test the gas piping, route the vent to spec, match the circuit to the load, and add expansion tanks, drain pans, or straps where required. Before we go, gas units are checked for draft and carbon monoxide. The result is an installation that is safe, sized correctly, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, you will hear it from us directly.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here's where to begin:

  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
