A typical tank water heater gives eight to twelve years of service in Royal Oak, MI, and that number drops with hard water, high pressure, or a tank nobody ever flushed. The end usually announces itself in one of a few ways: a slow leak from the bottom of the tank, rust in the hot water, sediment noise that gets louder by the month, and showers that go cold sooner every winter. A leak from the tank body is the clearest signal of all. Once the glass lining fails and the steel rusts through, no part can fix it, and a slow weep can turn into a flooded floor with no notice.

Picking the replacement is where most of the value is. A like-for-like gas or electric tank is usually the simplest and least expensive swap. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
